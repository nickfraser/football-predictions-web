EPLBench2026: Does This Thing Know Footy?

For years, AI benchmarks have asked whether models can translate1, code, do maths, and explain science. But one question has gone unanswered:

Does this thing know footy?

I couldn't care less whether GPT-5.6 Sol can prove some 50-year-old conjecture.2 What use is that in the real world? Can it confidently explain why Hull City are definitely going down, then completely reverse its position after they beat Man U 2–0 at home? Apparently nobody thought to test this.

Introducing EPLBench2026: the world's first benchmark for evaluating whether frontier AI models know footy.

While existing benchmarks waste precious compute on mathematics, multilingual reasoning and software engineering, EPLBench2026 tackles a substantially harder problem: predicting the final 2026–27 English Premier League table before a single ball has been kicked.

Each model submits a complete 1–20 league table before the season begins. No updates, no appeals, not even if Maresca panic-buys Enzo Fernandez on deadline day.

No final squads. No early-season form. No hindsight. Just zero-shot footy reasoning in its purest form: a model, its weights, and the courage to say Spurs are going down.

This is the real world.3 Injuries. Sackings. VAR. Pints. Pies. Questionable opinions delivered with absolute certainty.4 A benchmark with a higher xG-to-token ratio than anything currently on Hugging Face.

Best of all, EPLBench2026 takes an entire footy season to evaluate, ensuring that every entrant will be hopelessly obsolete by the time we know who won.

This is what we in machine learning call rigorous longitudinal evaluation.
